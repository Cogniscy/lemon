\section{Conclusion}
LEMON evaluates KG--text fidelity by opening graph predicates into weighted semantic factors. It does not replace sentence-level metrics, triple matching, or MINE-style node/edge scoring. Its role is diagnostic: when a relation fails, it identifies whether the failure concerns relation class, roles, direction, polarity, or evidence.

The experiments support this distinction. On WebNLG, the same factor representation supports forward coverage and reverse recoverability. The MINE-style comparison shows that nodes are easier to recover than edges, and the relation-deletion check shows that entity-label coverage can remain almost unchanged while relation coverage drops. Perturbation tests show that LEMON reacts to the semantic dimensions represented in each inventory, with biomedical predicates making polarity, direction, causality, and evidence especially visible.

The component and LLM analyses clarify the result. Role factors contribute most to diagnostic sensitivity under controlled damage. Label-only scoring can be more severe as a scalar detector, but it cannot explain whether the damage concerns role assignment, direction, polarity, or evidence. LLM judges provide a complementary view of residual recoverability; they do not replace expert validation.

The main contribution is a layer between whole-triple factuality and sentence-level similarity: LEMON gives a reproducible factor trace for graph--text errors that coarser scores collapse into one number. Future work should validate the predicate inventories with domain experts, strengthen the evidence layer with paraphrase and entailment signals, and repeat the reverse experiment with stronger text-to-KG extractors.

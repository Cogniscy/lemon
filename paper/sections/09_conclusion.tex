\section{Conclusion}
We introduced \lemonscore{}, a bidirectional factor-level semantic alignment framework for graph--text pairs. The method decomposes RDF predicates into weighted semantic factors and evaluates both KG$\rightarrow$Text coverage and Text$\rightarrow$KG recoverability. In a WebNLG pilot, expanded Forward \lemonscore{} reached 0.7915 and Reverse \lemonscore{} reached 0.7789, supporting the idea that the same predicate-factor representation can be used in both directions.

The MINE-style comparison showed that node information is easier to recover than edge information, and controlled perturbation calibration showed that relation deletion strongly reduces factor-aware scores while leaving exact entity-label coverage nearly unchanged. These findings position \lemonscore{} as a diagnostic layer between triple-level factuality and atomic-fact evaluation. Future work will replace synthetic adjudication with human expert annotation, evaluate against human coverage judgments, add stronger paraphrase evidence, and extend the framework to domain-specific graph--text data.

\section{Conclusion}
LEMON evaluates KG--text fidelity by opening graph predicates into weighted semantic factors. It does not replace sentence-level metrics, triple matching, or MINE-style node/edge scoring. Its role is diagnostic: when a relation fails, it identifies whether the failure concerns relation class, roles, direction, polarity, or evidence.

The experiments support this distinction. On WebNLG, the same factor representation supports forward coverage and reverse recoverability. The MINE-style comparison shows that nodes are easier to recover than edges, and the relation-deletion check shows that entity-label coverage can remain almost unchanged while relation coverage drops. The perturbation proxy describes prescribed damage to the dimensions represented in each inventory; it does not establish independent detection of that damage from text.

The component, expert-review, and LLM analyses clarify the result. Role factors contribute most to the prescribed damage proxy in the internal ablation. Label-only scoring can be more severe as a scalar detector, but it cannot explain whether the damage concerns role assignment, direction, polarity, or evidence. The expert trace review shows substantial agreement between LEMON and human factor labels, while LLM judges provide a complementary view of residual recoverability.

The main contribution is a layer between whole-triple factuality and sentence-level similarity: LEMON gives a reproducible factor trace for graph--text errors that coarser scores collapse into one number. Future work should scale the expert review to larger blind annotation sets, strengthen the evidence layer with paraphrase and entailment signals, and repeat the reverse experiment with stronger text-to-KG extractors.

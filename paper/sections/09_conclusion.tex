\section{Conclusion}
We introduced \lemonscore{}, a bidirectional factor-level diagnostic framework for graph--text pairs. The method decomposes relation predicates into weighted semantic factors and evaluates both KG$\rightarrow$Text coverage and Text$\rightarrow$KG recoverability. In a WebNLG pilot, expanded Forward \lemonscore{} reached 0.7915 and Reverse \lemonscore{} reached 0.7789, which supports the use of one predicate-factor representation in both directions.

The MINE-style comparison showed that node information is easier to recover than edge information. Controlled perturbation calibration showed that relation deletion strongly reduces factor-aware scores while leaving exact entity-label coverage nearly unchanged. The same factor inventory format was then instantiated for DrugProt and BC5CDR, where polarity, directionality, and causality are explicit relation dimensions. These findings position \lemonscore{} as a diagnostic layer between triple-level factuality and atomic-fact evaluation. Future work will replace synthetic adjudication with expert annotation, add stronger paraphrase evidence, and extend the biomedical transfer checks to full human-validated factor coverage studies.

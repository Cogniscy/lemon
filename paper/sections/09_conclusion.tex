\section{Conclusion}
We introduced \lemonscore{}, a bidirectional factor-level diagnostic framework for graph--text pairs. The method decomposes relation predicates into weighted semantic factors and evaluates both KG$\rightarrow$Text coverage and Text$\rightarrow$KG recoverability. In a WebNLG pilot, Forward \lemonscore{} reached 0.7915 and Reverse \lemonscore{} reached 0.7789, supporting the use of one predicate-factor representation in both directions.

The MINE-style comparison showed that node information is easier to recover than edge information. Controlled perturbation calibration showed that relation deletion strongly reduces factor-aware scores while leaving exact entity-label coverage nearly unchanged. The same factor inventory format was then instantiated for DrugProt and BC5CDR, where polarity, directionality, and causality are explicit relation dimensions. The exploratory LLM reliability probe added a second view: model judges often recovered residual factors from context, especially when deterministic perturbations removed nodes or swapped arguments. These findings position \lemonscore{} as a diagnostic layer between triple-level factuality and atomic-fact evaluation. Future work will replace provisional resources with expert annotation, add stronger paraphrase evidence, and extend the biomedical transfer checks to full human-validated factor coverage studies.

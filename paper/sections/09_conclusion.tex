\section{Conclusion}
\lemonscore{} evaluates graph--text alignment by opening relation predicates into semantic factors. This changes the unit of diagnosis. Instead of asking only whether a triple is present or whether two entity names overlap, the framework asks which roles, relation meanings, directions, polarity cues, and evidence factors survive from graph to text and back to graph.

The experiments show why this level of analysis is useful. On WebNLG, the same factor representation supports both forward coverage and reverse recoverability. The MINE-style comparison shows that node information is much easier to recover than edge information, and the relation-deletion sanity check shows that entity-label coverage can remain almost unchanged while factor-aware relation coverage drops substantially. Across WebNLG, DrugProt, and BC5CDR, the deterministic perturbation analysis further shows that the score reacts to the semantic dimensions represented in the inventory: roles, direction, polarity, causality, and evidence.

The ablation and LLM reliability analyses refine this conclusion. Role factors contribute most to sensitivity under controlled damage, while label-only scoring remains less interpretable because it cannot say which part of the predicate was lost. The LLM judges provide a complementary view: they often recover factors from residual context even when a deterministic perturbation marks them as damaged. This does not weaken the factor approach; it clarifies its use. Deterministic factor scoring measures intended, auditable semantic damage, while model-based judging can reveal residual recoverability.

Taken together, the results position \lemonscore{} as a diagnostic layer between whole-triple factuality metrics and text-driven atomic-fact evaluation. Its value is not that it replaces existing metrics, but that it explains relation-level failures that coarser scores collapse into a single number. The next step is to replace provisional inventories with expert-validated factor annotations and to connect the same audit trail to stronger paraphrase and text-to-graph evidence models.

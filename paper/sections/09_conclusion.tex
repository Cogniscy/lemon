
\section{Conclusion}
\lemonscore{} evaluates graph--text alignment by opening relation predicates into semantic factors. This changes the unit of diagnosis. Instead of asking only whether a triple is present or whether two entity names overlap, the framework asks which roles, relation meanings, directions, polarity cues, and evidence factors survive from graph to text and back to graph.

The experiments show why this unit matters. On WebNLG, the same factor representation supports both forward coverage and reverse recoverability. The MINE-style comparison shows that node information is much easier to recover than edge information, and the relation-deletion sanity check shows that entity-label coverage can remain almost unchanged while relation coverage drops. Across WebNLG, DrugProt, and BC5CDR, the perturbation analysis shows that the score reacts to the semantic dimensions represented in the inventory: roles, direction, polarity, causality, and evidence.

The ablation and LLM reliability analyses sharpen this picture. Role factors contribute most to sensitivity under controlled damage. Label-only scoring remains less interpretable because it cannot say which part of the predicate was lost. LLM judges add a complementary signal: they often recover factors from residual context even when a deterministic perturbation marks them as damaged. Deterministic factor scoring measures intended, auditable semantic damage; model-based judging can reveal residual recoverability.

The result is a diagnostic layer between whole-triple factuality metrics and text-driven atomic-fact evaluation. \lemonscore{} does not replace existing metrics. It explains relation-level failures that coarser scores collapse into a single number.

Next, the provisional predicate inventories should be validated by domain experts, with accept, partial, missing-factor, and direction-error rates reported separately. The lexical evidence layer should then be paired with stronger paraphrase, dense embedding, entailment, or QA signals while keeping the factor trace intact. The reverse experiment should also be repeated with stronger text-to-KG extractors, including KGGen-style and domain-specific pipelines. More distant extensions include modality, quantification, presupposition, and pragmatic effects; these layers require separate annotation and should not be inferred from the present results.

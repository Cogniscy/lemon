\section{Introduction}
Knowledge graphs encode information as typed relations between entities. In RDF-style graph-to-text generation, an input edge \((s,p,o)\) is not just a pair of entity labels: the predicate \(p\) specifies which relation holds, which semantic roles the arguments fill, and what kind of information must be preserved in text. Conversely, if a text is converted back into a graph, a useful reconstructed edge must preserve the relation meaning, not merely mention the same entities.

This distinction is often invisible to holistic automatic metrics. A sentence may mention \textit{Alan Bean} and \textit{Wheeler, Texas} while omitting that the relation is \textit{birthPlace}. It may paraphrase the relation, confuse it with a neighboring predicate, or preserve only the subject and object. Standard WebNLG evaluation uses reference metrics such as BLEU, METEOR, chrF++, TER, BERTScore, and BLEURT, and human evaluation criteria such as data coverage, relevance, correctness, and fluency \cite{gardent2017webnlg,castroferreira2020webnlg,gehrmann2021gem}. These criteria are essential, but they do not identify which internal semantic component of a predicate is covered or missing.

We propose \lemonscore{}, a factor-level semantic alignment framework for graph--text pairs. The core idea is to decompose each source predicate into typed, weighted semantic factors. For example, \textit{birthPlace} can be decomposed into a person-like subject factor, a biographical-relation factor, and a place-like object factor. \lemonscore{} then evaluates whether these factors are expressed in text or recovered in a reconstructed graph. This creates a diagnostic layer between binary triple-level metrics, such as FactSpotter, and text-directed atomic-fact metrics, such as FActScore \cite{zhang2023factspotter,min2023factscore}.

The framework is bidirectional. In the forward direction, KG$\rightarrow$Text, it measures whether a text covers the semantic factors of the input graph. In the reverse direction, Text$\rightarrow$KG, it measures whether a reconstructed graph preserves the semantic factors of the source graph. We also implement a \minebase{} node/edge baseline inspired by KGGen's Measure of Information in Nodes and Edges (MINE), which evaluates text-to-KG information retention through recovered nodes and relations \cite{mo2025kggen}. Finally, we calibrate metric behavior using controlled perturbations following the behavioral testing logic of CheckList and perturbation checklists for NLG metrics \cite{ribeiro2020checklist,sai2021perturbation}.

This paper asks four research questions. \textbf{RQ1:} Does factor-level coverage capture graph-to-text semantic preservation beyond entity-label overlap? \textbf{RQ2:} Does the same factor representation support both forward coverage and reverse recoverability? \textbf{RQ3:} How does factor-level recoverability relate to node/edge information retention in a MINE-style baseline? \textbf{RQ4:} Do the metrics behave predictably under meaning-destroying and meaning-preserving perturbations?

The contributions are: (1) a bidirectional, weighted factor-level formulation for graph--text semantic alignment; (2) a reproducible WebNLG pilot with factor decomposition resources, forward and reverse scores, and audit trails; (3) a MINE-style node/edge information baseline adapted to WebNLG graph--text pairs; and (4) controlled perturbation calibration showing that relation-level damage is detected by factor-aware metrics while simple entity-label overlap remains nearly unchanged. The study remains a pilot: factor decompositions are bootstrapped with rules and synthetic LLM adjudication, and the results are not claimed as human-validated gold.

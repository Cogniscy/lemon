\section{Limitations}
The most important limitation is the absence of human expert validation. The current factor decompositions are produced by deterministic rules, LLM candidates, and a synthetic LLM adjudicator. LLM-as-a-judge research documents systematic biases, so these decompositions must not be described as expert gold. The current resource should be treated as a provisional bootstrap.

The second limitation is lexical evidence. The deterministic scorer can find explicit labels and known cues, but it can miss paraphrases, implicit relations, and discourse-level entailment. A future version should add embedding or entailment evidence while keeping the same factor-level audit trail.

The third limitation is scale. The pilot covers 100 development examples and 308 edges. Larger WebNLG experiments and domain-specific datasets are needed. The next validation step should measure correlation between \lemonscore{} and human data-coverage judgments, and compare directly with FactSpotter, Data-QuestEval, and neural reference metrics.

Finally, expansion can inflate scores if fallback decompositions are too permissive. For this reason we report initial and expanded runs separately and mark expanded results as a deterministic baseline rather than as human-validated truth.


The reverse-direction experiment uses a lexical pseudo-extractor, not a full text-to-KG model. Likewise, the MINE-style baseline is an adaptation of node/edge information retention to WebNLG graph--text pairs. It should not be described as a full reproduction of KGGen's MINE benchmark. Adding a real KG extractor and an LLM-based fact inferability judge is future work.

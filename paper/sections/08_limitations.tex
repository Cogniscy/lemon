\section{Limitations}
The most important limitation is the absence of human expert validation. The current factor decompositions are produced by deterministic rules, LLM candidates, and a synthetic LLM adjudicator. LLM-as-a-judge research documents systematic biases, so these decompositions must not be described as expert gold. The current resource should be treated as a provisional bootstrap.

The second limitation is lexical evidence. The deterministic scorer can find explicit labels and known cues, but it can miss paraphrases, implicit relations, and discourse-level entailment. A future version should add embedding or entailment evidence while keeping the same factor-level audit trail.

The third limitation is scale. The pilot covers 100 development examples and 308 edges. Larger WebNLG experiments and domain-specific datasets are needed. The next validation step should measure correlation between \lemonscore{} and human data-coverage judgments, and compare directly with FactSpotter, Data-QuestEval, and neural reference metrics.

Finally, expansion can inflate scores if fallback decompositions are too permissive. For this reason we report initial and expanded runs separately and mark expanded results as a deterministic baseline rather than as human-validated truth.


The reverse-direction experiment uses a lexical pseudo-extractor, not a full text-to-KG model. Likewise, the MINE-style baseline is an adaptation of node/edge information retention to WebNLG graph--text pairs. It should not be described as a full reproduction of KGGen's MINE benchmark. The deterministic MINE-style score is a partial-credit node/edge composite, while the LLM-judged variant is a binary fact-recoverability score over retrieved subgraphs; these quantities should not be interpreted as interchangeable. The LLM-based fact inferability judge added in the MINE-style variant is still provisional and should be calibrated against human judgments and controlled perturbations. We therefore report valid judgment coverage, parse success, retry statistics, and deterministic agreement whenever the LLM-judged variant is used. Adding a real KG extractor remains future work.


\paragraph{Perturbation validity.}
The calibration perturbations are deterministic and template-like. They support causal sanity checks of metric behavior, but they do not cover the full range of natural paraphrases or factual errors. LLM-generated perturbations are left for future work because they require separate validation that the perturbation is truly meaning-preserving or meaning-destroying.

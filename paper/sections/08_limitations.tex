\section{Limitations}
The most important limitation is the absence of human expert validation. Current factor decompositions are produced by rules, LLM candidates, and synthetic LLM adjudication. This resource should be described as a bootstrap reference, not as expert gold. LLM-as-a-judge research documents systematic biases, so the factor schema and adjudicated decompositions require future human annotation and inter-annotator agreement studies \cite{zheng2023mtbench,gu2024llmjudge,gilardi2024llmjudges}.

The second limitation is lexical evidence. The deterministic scorer can find labels and known cues, but it can miss implicit relations, long-range entailment, and natural paraphrases outside the cue dictionary. Future versions should add embedding, entailment, or QA-based evidence while preserving the factor-level audit trail.

The third limitation is the reverse pipeline. The reconstructed graph is produced by a lexical pseudo-extractor, not by a full text-to-KG system. Likewise, our MINE-style baseline is an adaptation of KGGen's node/edge information idea to WebNLG graph--text pairs; it is not a reproduction of the original MINE benchmark. The deterministic MINE-style composite score, deterministic fact recoverability, and LLM fact recoverability measure related but distinct quantities.

The fourth limitation is perturbation validity. The controlled perturbations are deterministic and auditable, but some preserving perturbations are template-like. They support initial construct-validity checks, especially relation deletion, but they do not prove robustness to the full range of natural paraphrase. Larger studies should use curated paraphrase sets and human validation of perturbation labels.

Finally, the study is WebNLG-only and uses a small stratified pilot. This is appropriate for framework development, but claims about domain transfer, including medical or scientific text, require domain-specific graphs, expert factor schemas, and human gold annotations.

\section{Limitations and Error Sources}
Two error sources recur in the experiments. The first is inventory incompleteness: predicates without decompositions, or with weak lexical cues, cannot be scored reliably. The error-driven expansion step exposes this issue before it contaminates the metric, but fallback decompositions remain less informative than expert-designed ones. The second is relation recoverability. WebNLG texts often repeat entity labels, so node recovery is high, while edge and fact recovery are substantially lower. The factor trace is the practical output in such cases: a low score can be traced to a predicate, a factor role, a missing cue, or weak subject/object evidence.

The expert review is preliminary rather than a final gold-standard validation. It covers 35 factor rows and four completed submissions, so it is best read as a pilot trace-review check rather than a large blind annotation campaign. The factor inventories remain rule-based resources built from predicate labels, schema constraints, domain roles, lexical cues, and development-time candidates. Prior LLM-as-judge work also shows that model judges can scale evaluation while introducing preference, calibration, and reasoning biases; therefore the LLM probe is kept separate from the human trace review \cite{liu2023geval,zheng2023mtbench}.

The evidence layer is limited. It can find labels and known cues, but it can miss implicit relations, long-range entailment, and paraphrases outside the cue dictionary. A stronger implementation should add embedding, entailment, or QA-based evidence while preserving the factor trace.

The reverse pipeline uses a lexical pseudo-extractor rather than a full text-to-KG system. This keeps the experiment controlled, but the reverse scores should be read as metric-behavior diagnostics. Similarly, the MINE-style baseline adapts KGGen's node/edge information idea to graph--text pairs; it is not a reproduction of the original MINE benchmark.

LEMON also has a narrower scope than general semantic similarity. It assumes that a source graph, relation annotation, or predicate inventory is available. The present inventories cover roles, direction, polarity, causality, and evidence where those dimensions are encoded by the predicate resources. They do not model pragmatic phenomena such as irony, implicature, presupposition, stance, or rhetorical force. The perturbations are auditable but template-like; they support construct-validity checks for relation deletion, argument swap, and polarity flip, but they do not prove robustness to natural paraphrase or state-of-the-art biomedical extraction performance.

\section{Limitations}
The main limitation is the absence of human expert validation. The factor inventories are deterministic resources based on predicate labels, schema constraints, rules, and development-time LLM candidates. The three-judge LLM probe checks residual recoverability, but it is not human gold. Prior work reports LLM-judge biases, so future work should add expert annotation and inter-annotator agreement \cite{zheng2023mtbench,gu2024llmjudge,gilardi2024llmjudges}.

A second limitation is lexical evidence. The deterministic scorer can find labels and known cues, but it can miss implicit relations, long-range entailment, and paraphrases outside the cue dictionary. Future versions should add embedding, entailment, or QA-based evidence while preserving the factor-level audit trail.

The reverse pipeline is also limited. The reconstructed graph is produced by a lexical pseudo-extractor, not by a full text-to-KG system. Likewise, our MINE-style baseline adapts KGGen's node/edge information idea to graph--text pairs; it is not a reproduction of the original MINE benchmark.

Finally, the perturbations are deterministic and auditable, but still template-like. They support initial construct-validity checks, especially relation deletion, argument swap, and polarity flip, but they do not prove robustness to natural paraphrase. DrugProt and BC5CDR are used as transfer diagnostics for roles, directionality, and polarity; we do not claim state-of-the-art biomedical relation extraction performance.

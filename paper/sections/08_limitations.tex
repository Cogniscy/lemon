\section{Limitations}
The study does not include expert human validation. The factor inventories are deterministic resources based on predicate labels, schema constraints, rules, and development-time candidates. The LLM probe adds a semantic recoverability check, but model judgments are not a replacement for expert annotation. Prior LLM-as-judge work shows that model judges can scale evaluation but can also exhibit preference and reasoning biases; future work should add expert factor annotations and inter-annotator agreement \cite{liu2023geval,zheng2023mtbench}.

The deterministic evidence layer is also limited. It can find labels and known cues, but it can miss implicit relations, long-range entailment, and paraphrases outside the cue dictionary. A stronger implementation should add embedding, entailment, or QA-based evidence while preserving the factor-level audit trail.

The reverse pipeline uses a lexical pseudo-extractor rather than a full text-to-KG system. This makes the experiment controlled, but it also means that the reverse scores should be interpreted as metric-behavior diagnostics. Similarly, the MINE-style baseline adapts KGGen's node/edge information idea to graph--text pairs; it is not a reproduction of the original MINE benchmark.

The scope is also narrower than general semantic similarity. \lemonscore{} assumes that a source graph, relation annotation, or predicate inventory is available. It should therefore be read as a graph--text fidelity metric and as a restricted case of layered semantic comparison, not as a general-purpose text--text similarity metric. In particular, the current paper does not evaluate dense embedding baselines for arbitrary sentence pairs and does not claim to replace embedding-based retrieval or semantic textual similarity systems.

The present factor inventories cover relation-level roles, direction, polarity, causality, and evidence where those dimensions are encoded by the predicate resources. They do not model pragmatic phenomena such as irony, implicature, presupposition, stance, or rhetorical force. A future version could add such layers, but they are outside the current empirical scope.

Finally, the perturbations are deterministic and auditable, but still template-like. They support construct-validity checks for relation deletion, argument swap, and polarity flip, but they do not prove robustness to natural paraphrase. DrugProt and BC5CDR are used to test transfer of the factor format to biomedical roles, directionality, polarity, and causality; we do not claim state-of-the-art biomedical relation extraction performance.

\section{Error Analysis}
The experiments reveal two recurring error classes. The first is resource incompleteness: predicates without decompositions or with weak lexical cues cannot be scored reliably. The error-driven expansion step was designed to expose and reduce this problem. After expansion, all 308 development edges had decompositions, but fallback decompositions remain less informative than expert-designed ones.

The second class is relation recoverability. Node information is high because WebNLG verbalizations often repeat entity labels, but edge information is substantially lower. This pattern appears in the reverse experiment, in the MINE-style node/edge gap, and in the relation-deletion perturbation. It suggests that the difficult part of graph--text alignment is not usually naming entities, but preserving and detecting the relation that links them.

The factor-level trace is useful here. A low \lemonscore{} can be traced to a predicate, a factor role, a missing cue, or a weak subject/object evidence signal. This is the main practical advantage of the framework: it turns a scalar score into a debugging signal for datasets, generators, and extraction pipelines.

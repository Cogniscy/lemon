
\section{Error Analysis}
Two error classes recur across the experiments. The first is resource incompleteness. Predicates without decompositions, or with weak lexical cues, cannot be scored reliably. The error-driven expansion step exposes this problem before it contaminates the metric. After expansion, all 308 development edges had decompositions, but fallback decompositions remain less informative than expert-designed ones.

The second class is relation recoverability. Node information is high because WebNLG verbalizations often repeat entity labels, but edge information is substantially lower. The same pattern appears in the reverse experiment, in the MINE-style node/edge gap, and in the relation-deletion perturbation. The hard part of graph--text alignment is often not naming entities; it is preserving and detecting the relation that links them.

The factor trace is the practical output of the method. A low \lemonscore{} can be traced to a predicate, a factor role, a missing cue, or weak subject/object evidence. The score is therefore not just a number at the end of an evaluation run. It is a debugging signal for datasets, generators, and extraction pipelines.

\section{Experiments}
The experiments are organized by the four research questions from the introduction rather than by development stage.

\subsection{RQ1: Forward factor-level semantic coverage}
We first score WebNLG development texts using the synthetic adjudicated predicate decompositions and seed lexical cues. We then run an error-driven expansion: missing predicates are identified, rule-based or fallback decompositions are added, lexical cues are expanded, and coverage is recomputed. This measures how much of the initial low score comes from missing resources rather than from the text itself.

\subsection{RQ2: Bidirectional factor recoverability}
To test whether the factor representation is bidirectional, we reconstruct a graph from each WebNLG text with a deterministic lexical pseudo-extractor and compute Reverse \lemonscore{}. This score is compared with expanded Forward \lemonscore{}. A small forward--reverse gap would suggest that the same factor schema can support both KG$\rightarrow$Text coverage and Text$\rightarrow$KG recoverability.

\subsection{RQ3: MINE-style node/edge information retention}
We compare Reverse \lemonscore{} with a \minebase{} baseline inspired by KGGen's Measure of Information in Nodes and Edges. The deterministic variant reports node information, edge information, and a composite node/edge score. The LLM-judged variant evaluates binary fact recoverability on a fixed 50-fact subset using compact JSON-constrained judgments. We report parse success, deterministic agreement, and confidence to avoid treating LLM judgments as unqualified gold.

\subsection{RQ4: Controlled perturbation calibration}
We inject deterministic noise into text and reconstructed graphs. Destructive perturbations should reduce relevant scores; preserving perturbations should produce smaller changes. All perturbed examples, reconstructed graphs, and operation-level manifests are stored under a separate perturbation directory. To avoid misleading averages, text-side perturbations are used primarily to calibrate Forward \lemonscore{}, while graph-side perturbations are used primarily to calibrate Reverse \lemonscore{} and MINE-style recoverability.

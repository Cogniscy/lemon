\section{Results}
\subsection{From entity overlap to relation factors}
The first WebNLG pass shows that predicate resources determine whether relation-level evaluation is possible. With the initial factor dictionary, only 140 of 308 development edges could be scored, and the missing decomposition rate was 0.5455. After deterministic decomposition and cue expansion, all 308 edges had predicate factors and Forward \lemonscore{} rose from 0.3683 to 0.7915. This increase is not a claim that all WebNLG texts are faithful. It shows that a factor-level metric needs explicit predicate resources before it can diagnose text quality.

Lexical controls isolate the added signal. Exact entity-label overlap is already high at 0.9215 and does not change under predicate-resource expansion. Token cosine and Jaccard are fixed because the graph--text strings do not change. The factor score changes because it checks relation roles, predicate meanings, and lexical evidence, not just entity mentions.

The reverse run gives a second check on the same representation. A deterministic lexical pseudo-extractor recovered 390 of 408 nodes and 196 of 308 edges from the WebNLG texts. Reverse \lemonscore{} reached 0.7789, close to the expanded Forward score of 0.7915. This does not evaluate a general-purpose extraction system; it shows that the same predicate factors can be used coherently in both directions.

The MINE-style comparison shows where the difficulty lies. Its deterministic node/edge composite is 0.8028, but the aggregate is dominated by node recovery. On the locked 50-fact subset, node information is 0.9600, while edge information and deterministic fact recoverability are both 0.5400. The pattern is consistent: entity mentions are easier to preserve and recover than the relations that connect them.

A relation-deletion sanity check makes the same point more directly. Removing relation evidence changes exact entity-label coverage by only -0.0020, while Forward \lemonscore{} drops by -0.2106. Predicate cue coverage and MINE fact recoverability fall by -0.3636 and -0.3539. Preserving the entities is therefore not the same as preserving the predicate; relation-aware scores are needed to see this failure mode.

\input{tables/table_perturbation_sensitivity_drop}

The pattern is simple. Entity recall drops least because entity strings often remain when the relation is damaged. Triple-style and node/edge scores react more strongly, but collapse the error into one coarse decision. \lemonscore{} is most useful as a diagnostic decomposition: it reacts to argument swap and polarity flip when those dimensions are encoded in the inventory, and stays quiet when they are absent. The metric can diagnose only the predicate dimensions that the inventory makes explicit.

\input{tables/table_ablation_gain}

The ablation is an internal component analysis, not a ranking against \minebase{} or triple matching. It asks which factor groups drive \lemonscore{}'s diagnostic sensitivity. Removing participant-role factors produces the largest positive gain for the full model over the ablated variant, so roles are the main source of sensitivity to argument and node-level damage. Evidence, polarity, and direction have smaller effects, mostly in biomedical predicates where these factors are explicit. Label-only scoring can produce a larger scalar drop when surface labels disappear, but it cannot say whether the damage concerns roles, direction, polarity, or evidence.

\subsection{LLM factor judging as a complementary view}
The LLM probe evaluates semantic recoverability rather than deterministic damage. Each judge saw the source edge, factor inventory, and perturbed text, then labeled each factor as covered, partial, or absent. Table~\ref{tab:llm-reliability} summarizes the three judge batches.

\input{tables/table_llm_reliability}

The mean LLM factor score is 0.809, pairwise agreement is 0.597, and agreement with deterministic perturbation labels is 0.561. This moderate agreement is useful rather than surprising. Deterministic perturbations record intended damage; LLM judges estimate how much of the original factor remains recoverable from residual context. \lemonscore{} exposes relation-level failure modes, while model-based judging can show which failures remain inferable.

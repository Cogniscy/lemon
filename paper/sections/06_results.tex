\section{Results}
\subsection{Initial and expanded coverage}
The initial run scored 140 of 308 development edges. The missing decomposition rate was 0.5455, exact label coverage was 0.9215, predicate cue coverage was 0.3149, and \lemonscore{} coverage was 0.3683. This confirms that entity labels are often preserved in WebNLG references, while predicate-level semantic coverage cannot be established for many edges with the initial resource.

After error-driven expansion, all 308 edges were scored. Missing decomposition rate fell to 0.0. Predicate cue coverage increased to 0.6526 and \lemonscore{} coverage increased to 0.7915. The low-coverage edge count dropped from 191 to 41.

The increase should not be interpreted as proof that generated texts are universally faithful. It shows that the first score was strongly constrained by decomposition and cue coverage. Once all predicates receive decompositions and stronger cues, the scorer can measure relation-level evidence more completely.

\subsection{Comparison with lexical controls}
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} summarizes the baseline comparison. Exact entity-label overlap is high and unchanged by expansion. Graph--text token cosine and Jaccard are also unchanged, because they do not use predicate decompositions. Predicate cue coverage and \lemonscore{} coverage both increase after expansion, but \lemonscore{} remains distinct because it weights role-aware factors, not only lexical cues.

The main observation is that surface/entity overlap already appears high and does not explain the change in \lemonscore{}. The proposed metric is therefore sensitive to the completeness of predicate-level semantic decompositions and relation evidence, not only to whether entity names are repeated.


\subsection{Bidirectional comparison}
The reverse experiment produced a reconstructed graph with 390 of 408 nodes and 196 of 308 edges recovered. Reverse \lemonscore{} reached 0.7789, close to the forward expanded \lemonscore{} score of 0.7915. The deterministic MINE-style node/edge score was 0.8028, with node information 0.9692 and edge information 0.6364. This suggests that entity information is easy to recover in WebNLG, whereas relation recovery is the limiting factor.

The deterministic MINE-style score is slightly higher than reverse \lemonscore{}, but the metrics answer different questions. MINE-style scoring checks whether facts are recoverable from nodes and edges in the reconstructed graph. Reverse \lemonscore{} checks which weighted semantic factors of the original predicates survive reconstruction. The stabilized LLM-judged MINE-style variant is stricter: it estimates binary fact recoverability from the retrieved subgraph rather than the partial-credit node/edge composite. On the locked 50-fact subset, deterministic node information was 0.9600, deterministic edge information was 0.5400, the deterministic composite node/edge score was 0.7500, deterministic fact recoverability was 0.5400, and LLM fact recoverability was 0.5200. The judge agreement with deterministic fact recoverability was 0.9800 with 100\% parse success. Thus, the LLM judge does not currently overturn the deterministic recoverability signal; it mainly provides a stricter fact-level interpretation. The two scores are therefore complementary rather than substitutes.


\paragraph{Calibration under controlled noise.}
The calibration run is reported with separate text-side and graph-side summaries. This avoids averaging Forward LEMON over perturbations that affect only reconstructed graphs, and avoids averaging Reverse LEMON or MINE-style scores over purely text-side changes. Relation phrase deletion reduced Forward LEMON while leaving exact entity-label coverage almost unchanged, indicating that entity overlap alone is insensitive to relation-level damage. Graph-side node and edge deletion strongly reduced Reverse LEMON and MINE-style edge/fact recoverability, as expected. We additionally mark weak template perturbations, such as alias rewrites and predicate paraphrase templates, so their invariance behavior is not overinterpreted as fully validated natural paraphrase robustness.

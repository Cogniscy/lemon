\begin{abstract}
Graph--text evaluation has a common blind spot: a sentence may keep the right entity names while dropping the relation that makes a graph edge true. A faithful verbalization must preserve roles, direction, and predicate commitments; a reconstructed graph must recover them from text. We introduce \lemonscore{}, a bidirectional diagnostic framework that decomposes graph predicates into weighted semantic factors and evaluates both KG$\rightarrow$Text coverage and Text$\rightarrow$KG recoverability. The source graph remains the object of evaluation, but each predicate becomes an auditable set of roles, cues, polarity, direction, and evidence factors. On WebNLG, Forward \lemonscore{} reaches 0.7915 and Reverse \lemonscore{} reaches 0.7789. A \minebase{} comparison shows that node information is easier to recover than edge information. Controlled perturbations make the gap visible: deleting relation evidence reduces Forward \lemonscore{} by 0.211, while exact entity-label coverage changes by only 0.002. The same inventory format is applied to DrugProt and BC5CDR, where biomedical predicates make role, direction, polarity, and causality explicit. The study positions \lemonscore{} as an interpretable diagnostic layer for relation-level graph--text alignment; it is not a substitute for expert human validation.
\keywords{Graph-to-text evaluation \and Semantic alignment \and Knowledge graphs \and WebNLG \and Biomedical relation extraction \and Predicate decomposition}
\end{abstract}

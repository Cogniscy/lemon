\begin{abstract}
Graph-to-text systems should preserve the meaning of each input relation, not merely mention the correct entities or produce fluent text. Existing automatic metrics for RDF-to-text and data-to-text generation are mostly reference-based and holistic, while recent faithfulness metrics often operate at the level of whole triples. We propose \lemonscore{}, a diagnostic evaluation framework that decomposes source predicates into weighted semantic factors and checks how these factors are reflected in a verbalization. The method is source-directed: it starts from graph predicates and their argument roles, not from text-only atomic claims. We implement a reproducible pilot on a stratified WebNLG subset, construct a predicate inventory, bootstrap a controlled factor schema, generate and adjudicate predicate decompositions, and compute deterministic graph--text coverage. In the first coverage run, 140 of 308 development edges were scored and \lemonscore{} coverage was 0.368. After error-driven decomposition and lexical-cue expansion, all 308 edges were scored and coverage rose to 0.791, while exact entity-label overlap remained unchanged at 0.921. The results show that factor-level coverage exposes a diagnostic layer that surface overlap does not capture. The current study is a pilot: synthetic LLM adjudication and rule-based expansion are not treated as human gold.
\keywords{Graph-to-text evaluation \and Semantic coverage \and Knowledge graphs \and WebNLG \and Predicate decomposition \and LLM adjudication}
\end{abstract}

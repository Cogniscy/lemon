\begin{abstract}
Graph--text evaluation should not stop at checking whether the right entity names appear in a fluent sentence. A faithful verbalization must preserve the relation roles, direction, and semantic commitments encoded by graph predicates; a reconstructed graph must recover the same information from text. We propose \lemonscore{}, a bidirectional diagnostic framework that decomposes predicates into weighted semantic factors and evaluates both KG$\rightarrow$Text coverage and Text$\rightarrow$KG recoverability. The method occupies a middle ground between whole-triple factuality checks and text-driven atomic-fact metrics: it keeps the source graph as the object of evaluation, but opens each predicate into auditable components. On WebNLG, Forward \lemonscore{} reaches 0.7915 and Reverse \lemonscore{} reaches 0.7789, while a \minebase{} comparison shows that node information is much easier to recover than edge information. Controlled perturbations confirm the diagnostic value of the factor view: deleting relation evidence reduces Forward \lemonscore{} by 0.211 while exact entity-label coverage changes by only 0.002. The same inventory format is then applied to DrugProt and BC5CDR, where biomedical predicates make role, direction, polarity, and causality explicit. A three-judge LLM reliability probe gives a complementary view: model judges often recover residual factors from context, whereas deterministic perturbations measure intended semantic damage. The study supports \lemonscore{} as an interpretable diagnostic layer for relation-level graph--text alignment across open-domain and biomedical data; it is not a substitute for expert human validation.
\keywords{Graph-to-text evaluation \and Semantic alignment \and Knowledge graphs \and WebNLG \and Biomedical relation extraction \and Predicate decomposition}
\end{abstract}

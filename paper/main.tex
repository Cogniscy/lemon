\title{LEMON: Factor-Level Semantic Alignment for Graph--Text Pairs}
\titlerunning{LEMON for Graph--Text Alignment}

\begin{abstract}
Graph--text evaluation often misses a central failure mode: a sentence may preserve the entity names while changing or omitting the relation encoded by the graph edge. We introduce \lemonscore{} (Linguistically Enriched Measure of Ontological Normalization), a diagnostic metric for bidirectional knowledge-graph (KG)--text fidelity. \lemonscore{} decomposes each graph predicate into weighted semantic factors and evaluates both KG$\rightarrow$Text coverage and Text$\rightarrow$KG recoverability. Its goal is not to be the most severe scalar detector. Instead, it explains relation-level errors by localizing them to argument roles, relation class, direction, polarity, or textual evidence. On WebNLG, Forward \lemonscore{} reaches 0.792 and Reverse \lemonscore{} reaches 0.779. A \minebase{} comparison confirms that node information is easier to recover than edge information, while worked examples and controlled perturbations show why a factor trace is more informative than a single edge-failure label. The same inventory format is applied to DrugProt and BC5CDR, where biomedical predicates make role, direction, polarity, and causality explicit. A pilot review with four expert reviewers yields 85.0\% exact agreement between LEMON and expert factor labels, and LEMON matches the expert majority on 27/28 rows with a $\geq$3/4 majority. Code and reproducibility materials are available at \url{https://github.com/Cogniscy/lemon}.
\keywords{Graph-to-text evaluation \and Semantic alignment \and Knowledge graphs \and WebNLG \and Biomedical relation extraction \and Predicate decomposition}
\end{abstract}
